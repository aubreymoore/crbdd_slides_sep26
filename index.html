- Feeding damage to coconut palms is caused only by adults which bore into the crown to feed on sap. - Beetles are not true defoliators. The damage is caused to developing leaves inside the crown as beetle bore through them on their way to the sap. - When the fronds emerge from the crown several weeks later, they have these distinctive v-shaped cuts which can be seen for several hundred meters. - Tree mortality occurs only when the single meristem at the base of the the crown and this rarely happens at low population levels. - It is not correct to refer to palms like those in these images as being "infested". CRB adults feed about once per month, feeding in a bore hole only for a few days. - Referring to these trees as "infested" is incorrect. Chances are that no beetles where inside them when these photos were taken. - All of these trees, accept for the dead one in the background, would have survived if further damage was stopped. - Dead trees should be removed and processed so they don't become breeding sites for generations of grubs. - But there is an exception to this. Leaving some dead palms standing is recommended where CRB populations are being controlled with OrNV because these breeding sites provide an important transmission site for the virus.

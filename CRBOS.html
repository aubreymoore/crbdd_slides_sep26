Improving Access to Data, Information, and Knowledge About Coconut Rhinoceros Beetle

Abstract. Coconut rhinoceros beetle (CRB), Oryctes rhinoceros (Linnaeus, 1758) has been studied for a long time. It was one of the first beetles described by Linnaeus, and Charles Darwin did research on it the 1800s. Prior to that, this insect was well known by people living within its native range (spanning regions from Pakistan and India through Southeast Asia to the Philippines and parts of southern China). Despite this long history, knowledge about CRB is sparse: We do not know how to eradicate this pest, stop it from spreading, or sustainably control the damage it causes. There is an urgent need to identify knowledge gaps and do targeted research to fill those gaps. In this presentation I will present some ideas from the Open Science and the Biodiversity Informatics communities to improve sharing data, information and knowledge about CRB with the goal of speeding up discovery of new knowledge which will enable better management of this pest.

Open Science
    The goal of open science is to make research, data, and software free and open to everyone so that scientific progress is faster, fairer, and easier to trust.
Core Pillars of Open Science
  • Open Access: Making peer-reviewed publications and research literature freely available to everyone, without paywalls.
  • Open Source & Methods: Sharing computer code, software, lab protocols, and study designs transparently.
  • Open Data: Publishing raw and processed research data in public repositories so findings can be verified and reused.
Core Objectives of Open Science
  • Make research transparent: Share data, code, and papers openly so anyone can check the work.
  • Speed up discovery: Help scientists build on each other's ideas quickly without paying for hidden journal access.
  • Reduce inequality: Give researchers and students in poor or developing countries the same tools and information as wealthy nations.
  • Build public trust: Let the public see how science works and encourage participation of citizen scientist to increase confidence in proven facts.
Impediments to Increasing the CRB Knowledgebase
  • Knowledge level: Many journal articles are published behind pay walls
  • Information level: Most CRB grant reports and technical reports are not published or shared publicly; online resources disappear or become inaccessible
  • Data level: Raw data from CRB surveys are not shared and results are not comparable because data standards are not being used
Suggestions for Removing
Impediments to Increasing the CRB Knowledgebase
  1. Establish and maintain an online reference library for CRB.
  2. Save CRB occurrence records in Darwin Core Archives.
